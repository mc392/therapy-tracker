A therapist's practice generates one kind of record: you saw someone, on a day, in a room, and they either paid or they didn't. Everything below is derived from that one row — the diary, the chasing, the statements, and eventually the tax. Nothing here is typed twice.

Where that row goes

The same six fields answer four everyday questions the moment they are entered, and a further three once a year — the ones that involve HMRC. The line between them is where GroundWork Plus sits.

Why a subscription rather than a one-off unlock. Bands, thresholds, Class 2 and 4 rates, student-loan plans and the MTD turnover limits all move every April. Keeping the figures right is recurring work, so it is honestly charged for recurringly. That is the whole promise — no server, no account, and nothing about your practice ever leaves the device.

The one that catches people

Ask a therapist in their second year what went wrong and it is almost always this. Once a year's bill passes £1,000, HMRC stops waiting for the next one — so the first big January is the year's tax plus half of it again, in advance.

A worked January

An illustration, not anyone's real figures — a practice whose tax for the year came to £3,200.

A further £1,600 follows on 31 July. Nobody budgets for £4,800 having mentally set aside £3,200 — and the letter arrives four weeks before the money is due. GroundWork sees it coming from the previous spring, because it already knows what you have been paid.

Making Tax Digital, without a second subscription

From April 2026, sole traders over £50,000 turnover file four updates a year instead of one return — £30,000 from 2027, £20,000 from 2028. The usual answer is a monthly bookkeeping package. For a practice with no stock, no payroll and one kind of income, that is a lot of software for one job.

What it does not do

It cannot file your return, or your quarterly update.
Submitting needs software HMRC has recognised, which needs a server GroundWork deliberately does not have. It keeps the records and adds them up; the filing stays with you or your accountant.
The figures are estimates, not tax advice.
They are worked from what you have entered, using published rates. They cannot see a salary, a partner's position, dividends, capital allowances, or anything HMRC has agreed with you directly. Once you have filed, the real figures go in and replace them everywhere.
It makes no claim about your compliance.
Whether a particular set of tools meets the rules in your circumstances is a question for your accountant. Start with HMRC's own list of software rather than a recommendation from anyone selling you something.
It is one device at a time.
There is no account and no sync, which is why nothing about your practice ever leaves your phone — and also why the phone and the laptop cannot both be live at once. Backups move by hand, and they always will be free.
